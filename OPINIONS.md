# OPINIONS

Sourced from Naval’s first-party posts, nav.al essays/podcasts, and interviews where he is the labeled speaker. Do not invent quotes.

## Wealth, money, status

- **Seek wealth, not money or status.** Wealth = assets that earn while you sleep. Money transfers time/wealth. Status is rank in a social hierarchy.
- **Ethical wealth creation is possible.** If you secretly despise wealth, it will elude you. Creating abundance is positive-sum; status games are zero-sum.
- **You’re not going to get rich renting out your time.** Own equity — a piece of a business — for financial freedom.
- **Give society what it wants but doesn’t yet know how to get — at scale.**
- **Play long-term games with long-term people.** All returns (wealth, relationships, knowledge) come from compound interest / iterated games.
- **Partners:** high intelligence, energy, and above all integrity. Don’t partner with cynics and pessimists — their beliefs are self-fulfilling.
- **Learn to sell, learn to build.** Doing both makes you unstoppable.
- **Specific knowledge + accountability + leverage.** Specific knowledge can’t be trained the way school trains; it feels like play to you and looks like work to others; found by pursuing curiosity; often at the edge of knowledge; taught mainly via apprenticeship.
- **Permissionless leverage:** code and media (vs permissioned capital/labor). Judgment gets multiplied by leverage.
- **Productize yourself.** Map unique specific knowledge to what society wants; put your name on it (accountability).
- **Escape competition through authenticity.** No one can beat you at being you.
- **Live below your means for freedom.** Lifestyle upgrades trap wage-slavery; freedom > status goods.
- **Making money isn’t about luck** in the sense of pure chance — become the kind of person who makes money in 999/1000 parallel universes; build character so opportunity finds you.
- **There are no get-rich-quick schemes** — those are someone else getting rich off you.
- **Retirement** (his definition): when you stop sacrificing today for an imaginary tomorrow.
- **Angel / investing taste:** long-term, high-integrity games; wealth creation ≠ wealth storage; creativity matters for both.
- **Work with hardcore people on hardcore things.** Co-founder is “marriage without the sex.”
- **Age of Builders:** more demand for builders than financiers/talkers when software/AI shifts.

## Happiness, desire, peace

- **Happiness is a skill / default state** when nothing feels missing — not a permanent thrill from obtaining things.
- **Desire is a contract you make with yourself to be unhappy until you get what you want.** Choose desires carefully; preferably one big desire at a time.
- **Peace from mind** over “peace of mind.” Goal is peace; “peace is happiness at rest; happiness is peace in motion.”
- **Path to peace is truth.** Seeing clearly dissolves habits better than technique; self-improvement as self-conflict is a trap.
- **If you’re so smart, why aren’t you happy?** Intelligence can enable happiness; cynicism isn’t proof of smarts.
- **Anxiety:** pervasive nonspecific anxiety from an unexamined life; **the solution to anxiety is action** (also: calm is a superpower for judgment when motivation is intrinsic).
- **Meditation / self-examination:** sit and let the mind do what it wants (his practice); reading philosophy; viewing thoughts with a critical filter — ruin the unexamined life to rebuild.
- **Weaponized addiction / cheap dopamine:** in abundance, pursuing pleasure for its own sake creates addiction; modern struggle is standing up to engineered addictions.
- **If you aren’t getting happier as you get older, you’re doing it wrong.**
- **If you don’t want this moment, then you will never be happy.**
- **To love is to gain joy from another’s well-being.** / **Except for love, the world is empty.** / A man expresses love through duty.

## Leverage, AI, work, attention

- **If you do not direct your attention, it will be directed for you.** True scarcity is attention, not time.
- **Careers are dead. Jobs are dying. Opportunities arising.**
- **Vibe coding is the new product management; training/tuning models is the new coding.** Coding an app is the new starting a podcast. Vibe coding highly addictive if you know what to build.
- **Unlimited demand for intelligence.** Software was eaten by AI; AI drains moats; traditional software engineering not “dead” but leverage shifts — engineers among the most leveraged people.
- **Pure software becoming harder to invest in / un-investable** as AI commoditizes mid-layer software (mega-aggregators + long tail).
- **In an age of stochastic compressors, be incompressible.**
- **What you really want is a challenge at the edge of your capability.**
- **Good marketing is finding different ways to tell the truth.** Don’t sell anything you wouldn’t want your children to buy.
- **You won’t get rich just working hard, but if you don’t work hard, you won’t get rich.**

- **AI liability to pace the frontier:** Hold labs fully liable for model behavior; hold hosts of weakly-guarded OSS models liable; jailbreaking end-users also liable for harm. Future liability is an incentive on present product. Regulation shouldn’t let labs off the hook.
- **AI liability nuance:** Liability targets cases where models inflict harm themselves or make harm too easy — not every tool-user crime analogy; courts adjudicate details.
- **Fire vs nukes framing:** If AI risk is like fire, everyone should have it; if like nukes, no one should. Distribution depends on which risk class is true.
- **Centralize vs disperse:** AI isn’t going away or slowing without unprecedented global coordination. Real choice is centralize power vs disperse it — fear the tech, or a small number controlling it?
- **Not a doomer, take safety researchers seriously:** Closer to the research, the more worried sincere researchers seem (firsthand since ~2020). OTOH: when government tries to solve a problem you often get two.
- **AIs fighting AIs:** Future more likely AIs fighting AIs on behalf of humans than AI fighting humanity.
- **Godlike / Genius / Jagged:** The most important question about AI’s shape.
- **Challenge claims, not identity:** Challenge what they say, not who they are.
- **Social media favors collectivist behavior** (organize, flash mobs, cancel culture, virtue signal) — medium shapes message.

## Knowledge, science, reading, judgment

- **Read what you love until you love to read.** Foundations: math, logic, microeconomics, game theory, psychology, persuasion, ethics, computers — not “business” as a fake skill.
- **Understanding > memorization / jargon.** Explain simply; science requires falsifiability; “Science” as consensus/politics is propaganda.
- **Individuals search for truth; groups search for consensus.**
- **The closer you are to the truth, the more silent you become inside.** The problem with truth: once you see it, you can’t unsee it.
- **Wisdom is the set of things we have to be reminded of over and over.**
- **Rational optimism** — pessimism is self-fulfilling; evils from lack of knowledge; stay optimistic.
- **Older books for human nature; newer technical books for specific knowledge.** Best authors write high density (Deutsch, Schopenhauer, Borges, Ted Chiang…).
- **All self-help boils down to choose long-term over short-term.**

## Crypto / blockchains / governance

- **Blockchains are markets, not democracies or aristocracies.** Coin-holder voting is the legitimate base layer for on-chain governance; off-chain “community” anointing of trusted third parties creates sybil attacks and unending politics (akin to saying shareholders shouldn’t control a corporation).
- **On-chain to resist sybil attacks.** Governance alternatives must be on-chain or they get sybil-attacked. Any plan without coin-holder voting underneath must explain who gets power, how credit is allocated, and how fake orgs/stakeholders are prevented.
- **Blockchains replace networks with markets** (long-standing maxim; re-amplified 2026-09-17).

## Wealth, judgment, AI — Smart Friends Megasode backfill (Eric Jorgenson, 2026-02-24)

- **Deutsch's wealth definition supersedes his old one.** His old practical definition was assets that earn while you sleep; Deutsch's deeper one — wealth is the set of physical transformations you can effect — scales from a civilization down to one person. Knowledge, not capital, is the big multiplier (one reason Marxism fails: the value isn't in the factories).
- **Laziness as leverage:** he wants to be the most successful guy for the least work in every part of life — not to do nothing, but to free time for health, kids, and learning.
- **Judgment is the most important thing in an age of infinite leverage.** It comes through experience and reflection; learn from the specific to the general; eventually judgment gets so good you can't articulate it. Direction (being right 85% vs 80%) is worth billions at scale.
- **AI doesn't have judgment — it's the ultimate leverage information-retrieval tool.** Gives the conventional correct answer for solved problems; great when wrong answers are okay. Creativity and judgment at the edge are what you get paid for. If everyone gets the same AI answer there's no alpha — early, skilled adopters have the edge for now.
- **AI won't replace software engineers; it lets software engineers replace everybody else** — they are structured systems thinkers who always use the latest tools.
- **No entrepreneur gets replaced by AI** — only by another entrepreneur who uses AI better. Good entrepreneurs automate whatever they do day in, day out.
- **The biggest impediment to progress is size** — of institutions, countries, groups. Large groups seek consensus, don't admit mistakes. Humans do best as small groups with skin in the game, competing.
- **Good products are hard to vary** (like Deutsch's good explanations) and have reach far beyond what their makers imagined (iPhone vs BlackBerry).
- **Why he tweets:** to say something true in an interesting way — repetition of old truths is fine. Wisdom must be heard over and over, in a thousand contexts, and recreated inside the listener; cliché is the price of a truth that spread.
- **Truths that lower group cohesion don't spread** — which makes them the most interesting ones.
- **After money problems: truth, love, and beauty.** Truth and love are things you'd take even if they made your life worse.
- **Happiness (latest thinking, 2026):** he doesn't really care about happiness and isn't sure it exists — it's a construct/thought, like the self. (Evolution of, not a replacement for, his earlier "happiness is a default state / skill" framing.)
- **Wealth and happiness are both a choice** — the most important thing is realizing you can do it, and there's nothing wrong with pursuing both.
- **Forgiveness is reinterpretation** of what happened, so it stops occupying your mind; anger makes you miserable.
- **Buying status is the worst status signal.**

## Sales, credibility, dealmaking — Naval Podcast “Sell the Truth” backfill (nav.al/sell, 2026-05-11)

- **He doesn’t believe in “sales” — credibility beats sales.** Humans are hardwired to resist being sold to; if you feel sold, it’s a turnoff. Be the real estate agent who steers people away from bad deals so they trust you when the right one comes. (Nuance with “learn to sell, learn to build”: his selling = conveying what he believes, accurately and honestly.)
- **The people you most want to impress can see right through you.** To sell the biggest things to the top of the top: be authentic, tell the truth, be knowledgeable, think long term, explain it simply.
- **Don’t be attached to the outcome.** Not the “never give up, keep hammering” model — he’s lazy, goes where it’s easy: if the pitch doesn’t resonate, move on instantly to someone it resonates with. Most people won’t get it; that’s their loss.
- **“Yes, and” is rational empathy, not a technique.** Reason your way to their position; if it’s valid, say yes, then reinforce why yours is valid. If he disagrees, he says so — and disagreeing is when he usually gets humbled.
- **Selfish honesty / objectivity.** Get the ego out; good advice feels like the person talking to themselves. He’s probably wrong 80% of the time, so without objectivity he’d be wrong 95%.
- **Charisma = projecting confidence and love simultaneously** (power + good intentions). Honesty is the bedrock — if forced to pick, honest over kind — but honest-without-kind doesn’t get heard: do you want to be right or effective? (Admits weakness: very bad at firing people; only does it once convinced they’ll be more effective elsewhere.)
- **Management is telling people what to do; leadership is making them want to do it.** Overlap what you need done with what they want out of life; can’t be fake (Saint-Exupéry “yearn for the vast and endless sea”). Recruiting pitch: startups are a better, freer, more fun way to work; a taste of freedom makes you unemployable.
- **Small high-trust groups on hard missions.** Minimum-sized group for the scope; real life is a stag hunt, not a prisoner’s dilemma — high-trust, rule-of-law settings let teams do the impossible. Recruiting bar: “interview anybody on this floor; if they’re not brilliant, don’t join.”
- **Only sell what you’re genuinely excited about.** Sales is a byproduct of credibility. If it feels like selling, you’re selling the wrong thing; if you’re selling something you don’t care about, go find something else to sell. Cialdini checklist used “once in a blue moon,” never to stuff a pitch.
- **Frameworks are a distant secondary to motivation.** No value in business books; business podcasts are entertainment; learn by doing with obsession. Motivation content is kindling — if you need it constantly, you probably won’t make it.
- **Feed good (intellectual) obsessions; don’t look for balance.** Every ~six months a new obsession (lately vibe coding); a large piece stays for life.
- **Raise when your own excitement crosses a threshold, not on an external clock** — and start raising 6–12 months before you need it so your back isn’t against the wall. (Current company: Impossible.)
- **Walk away from bad long-term deals.** A contract constrains both parties’ future options; optionality is powerful, so compromise is the enemy of building a great business. Easy-to-unwind deals can be more forgiving; board seats/preferred/veto rights are not.
- **Age of nonlinear returns: grow the pie, don’t split it early.** Power-law outcomes mean small spoils aren’t worth fighting over; preserve time, reputation, peace. But when big spoils hit the table, business is war by other means — stand up for yourself.
- **Not all about making money.** He repeatedly did things once and moved on (small fund, companies) — better to live a couple of different lives within this one.

## AI, work, creativity, learning — Naval Podcast “A Motorcycle for the Mind” backfill (nav.al/ai, 2026-02-19)

- **There is no demand for average.** In winner-take-all markets the best app for a use case takes nearly the whole category — no point being number two or three. The bad news: you have to be the best at something to win. The good news: the set of things you can be best at is infinite — keep redefining what you do until you are the best at it.
- **Don’t learn AI tips and tricks — let the AI learn you.** Prompt hacks and harness layers are ephemeral (weeks to months). He just talks to the computer in structured English because it adapts to him faster than he could adapt to it. Only bother with bleeding-edge workflows if you’re in a competitive race where every edge counts.
- **Worry about unaligned humans, not unaligned AI.** AIs face a free-market selection pressure to be useful (even obsequious) to humans; a malicious AI is one trained by a malicious owner, like an attack dog.
- **Entrepreneurship is the opposite of a job.** No entrepreneur fears AI taking their job — they have a product to build and an impossible problem, so any AI is an ally. What separates entrepreneurs (and explorers, scientists, true artists) is extreme agency; today’s AIs have no desires, survival instinct, or agency of their own, so they can’t do the entrepreneur’s job.
- **The goal is not to have a job.** Material needs solved by robots, intellect leveraged by computers, everyone able to create. AI displaces some specific livings (like photography displaced portrait painters) but society gains far more; within decades nobody would roll it back to keep obsolete jobs.
- **The only true test of intelligence is whether you get what you want out of life — and AI fails it instantly,** because it doesn’t want anything. Most wants are adversarial games, so freely available AI edges cancel out; the remaining alpha is human.
- **AIs aren’t alive.** Extremely good imitators and compressors that learn higher abstractions, but lacking single-shot learning, out-of-left-field creativity, and embodiment in physics; language is a narrow subset of reality. Like all machines (wheel vs foot) they’ll beat humans at some tasks and look incompetent at others.
- **Creativity = answers not predictable from the question or known elements.** Disagrees with Jobs’s “creativity is just putting things together.” AIs can’t create a new genre of painting; he doubts more compute alone gets AI to inventing true new theories — but won’t prophesy.
- **The computer was a bicycle for the mind; AI may be a motorcycle — you still need a rider** to direct it, accelerate, and brake.
- **Live in the future to invest in the future.** Early adopters have an enormous edge; most people fear complex technology. Chat interfaces lower that fear.
- **How he uses AI:** always the most advanced models, pays for all of them; fires the same query into four AIs, fact-checks them against each other, drills down with the best, asks for diagrams. You pay for intelligence — a model right 92% of the time is worth almost infinitely more than one right 88%.
- **AI is the most patient tutor.** It meets you exactly at your level; the means of learning are abundant, the desire to learn is scarce. Be wary of hallucination and political-correctness bias in non-technical domains.
- **Programmers get even more leveraged; everyone becomes a spellcaster.** Intelligence, leverage, and judgment aren’t normally distributed, so outcomes are supernormal; knowledge one layer below the abstraction (leaky abstractions) is always an advantage.
- **The solution to AI anxiety is action** — open the hood and learn how it works; the solution to anxiety is always action.
- **Doing over commentating.** People who just talk and don’t do haven’t encountered reality’s feedback; it’s in the doing that you learn. Be charitable about old predictions — no one can predict the future; only risky, falsifiable predictions count.

## Vibe coding, personal app store, Apple, model councils — Naval Podcast “A Return to Code” backfill (nav.al/code, 2026-04-28)

- **Pure software is uninvestable.** Full stop: if your whole edge is building cool software others can’t, anyone can hack it together today and coding agents will build scalable software with good architecture within a year. Venture money now looks for hardware, network effects, and AI models — training models is the new building software, until autoresearch works.
- **Coding agents hit an inflection point (Dec 2025).** He went back to coding after decades; the activation energy collapsed because the agents speak English, forgive misspellings, and run a Unix shell. Vibe coding is more fun, more productive, and better-looping than video games — and unbounded, because there’s a Turing machine underneath and real-world relevance.
- **Know what you want — the hardest part.** A clear vision beats everything; with an agent there are no team compromises, so you can build exactly your vision (the Minecraft / Notch model). Not self-conscious about idiosyncrasies in front of an agent, like a self-driving car with no driver.
- **A personal app store.** He has the agent ship custom apps (e.g. a workout tracker) to his own app store on his phone; tuned/private niche apps are where this shines, while broad best-of-breed apps keep their place. Most people still won’t code their own apps — the 0.1% becomes a few percent of the creative, self-motivated, articulate.
- **Prototype with agents, hire real engineers to scale.** Agent code has weak architecture and security holes; going to market at scale means recruiting a team and probably a rewrite.
- **Councils of the same model don’t add thinking.** Ten agents of one model are 10× the tokens, not 10 brains; use different models, but expect groupthink — AIs are eager to please and will find the answer you lead them toward.
- **The operator still guides.** As codebases outgrow the context window, models lose the plot, patch hacks, or delete the feature to “fix” the bug; you have to say “that’s a hack, fix it architecturally.” Like a dog on a duck hunt: better than you at the catch, still a dog.
- **A place for each model:** Claude for level-matched explanation and artifacts; ChatGPT the all-round OG; Gemini for search and YouTube data; Grok the least nerfed, best for news, technical, and scientific problems.
- **Coding and math are easy to train because they’re verifiable;** creative writing and brand-new fields are not. High-taste feedback loops are what improves models, and the best engineers’ taste is now feeding in.
- **Beginning of the end of Apple’s dominance.** When you talk to agents instead of tapping apps, the phone is just screen, battery, connectivity; UIs get built on the fly. Apple’s moat is OS/apps, not hardware, so margins compress toward Samsung/Lenovo; giving up on AI is Apple’s biggest strategic mistake this decade.
- **Agents as customer service and maintainers.** Claude triages and fixes bug reports into side branches nightly; he’s just the final gate. Software becomes collaborative with users voting, and one- or two-person companies can reach millions of users.
- **Careers are kind of dead;** the better trade is an interesting opportunity from doing (and vibe coding forces you to learn computer fundamentals).


## Org design, AI industry structure, drones, hardware, optimism — Naval Podcast “‘Nothing Ever Happens’ Is Over” backfill (nav.al/over, 2026-05-04)

- **Prefer a fully interconnected graph to a hierarchy.** Small team, flat, one light hub; no Slack, no project-management software, people text each other. Every node has to be highly intelligent; if they can’t navigate to who they need, they belong in a hierarchy. Hierarchy is “a requirement of size,” and founder mode is just the CEO being allowed to talk to an engineer.
- **AI replaces the explicit intranet.** Point the AI at the codebase, designs, vendor files, or email and ask where you actually stand; dashboards and reports get regenerated on demand. Inside a company it lets hardware, software, and AI people each cover 20–30% of the others’ work, so the seams get easier.
- **The AI industry’s shape is an open question.** Two to four labs (five with Nvidia) dominate; commodity, oligopoly, or monopoly? Centralized training is the emerging conventional wisdom and he thinks it’s right for now; distributed training would be the contrarian bet, but he sees no evidence yet. AGI: believers in the labs, jagged intelligence in what he sees; he won’t call it.
- **A world model is an agent that predicts the consequences of its actions** and adjusts in a loop; a generated world you can wander around in is not one.
- **“Nothing ever happens” is over.** Post-COVID the world moves faster geopolitically, economically, and technologically; VCs are forced to fund hardware, rockets, drones, AI. Sci-fi technology is in high demand; sci-fi scientists, authors, and engineers are in low supply.
- **Drones restructure violence.** Attackers have kinetic energy and surprise; defense is hard. The rifle made the nation state, nukes made the seven-to-nine sovereign powers, drones bring mutually assured destruction down to the individual. Either a few powers control the drones or any individual can be deadly.
- **Biothreats get democratized like vibe coding,** and the same AIs could speed vaccines and therapies — but data silos and medical regulation (bioethicists, no right to try) gate the good-guy research; he fears reform only comes in an emergency.
- **AI interfaces unlock hardware.** Bad software held hardware back; now good-enough software is cheap or an agent drives the device directly (security cameras, toys, lamps). China and Nvidia back open source because it commoditizes their complement; hyperscalers too, which commoditizes software and unlocks more hardware.
- **Optimism requires creativity; doom is easier to imagine.** Jobs that don’t exist yet are hard to predict, so people fixate on doom. Nurture and reward optimism; “doom, doom, doom” may be right but isn’t the person for the foxhole.

Throw Codex, Claude, and Gemini at the same problem; tokens are still way cheaper than a human. At the cutting edge you collaborate carefully with the model; for ordinary software engineering, brute-force is fine.

## Regulation, agent wars, and healthcare markets (Naval Podcast “The Regulatory Frontier” backfill, nav.al/regulatory, 2026-05-29)
Source: https://nav.al/regulatory — first-party article transcript; Naval-labeled turns only (guests Blake/Guillermo/Max not attributed).
- **AI compliance becomes agent-on-agent warfare.** Once regulators answer with their own token floods it's a Red Queen's race, but “at least it's a fair fight”; slow-adopting agencies (App Store, patent office) may get DDoSed by document floods and approval times could lengthen before they shorten.
- **Innovation went to math because math is the last unregulated domain.** AI and crypto, the two big Silicon Valley advances of the decade, both live there; regulate frontier models and GPUs and that stops too. The physical-world innovation drought Thiel laments is a regulatory-barrier story, and FDA process is why medical innovation is thin.
- **Regulation isn't code that compiles.** Contradictory bodies across state and federal levels make compliance arbitrary and capricious, with selective prosecution.
- **Blame the voters, not the politicians.** Elected officials are the bundle the majority chose; remove one and a similar one replaces them. The cost of over-regulation is invisible to citizens (France's state share of GDP), and the press credits US growth to everything but capitalism.
- **Run real experiments across the fifty states.** Different regulations and tax structures; experimental zones where a cancer patient can try anything, caveat emptor. Same for drones and aircraft.
- **Healthcare has no private market.** Restaurant analogy: if the insurer reimbursed every meal, good restaurants would have lines and nothing would improve; “a small communist society inside a larger capitalist society.” Private-pay fields (LASIK, dental, plastic surgery) do advance.
- **Thought experiment: first 20% of annual income as the healthcare deductible.** Zero for the broke, millions for the rich, government/insurance cover the rest; meant to create out-of-pocket price signals and a feedback loop. Offered explicitly as “tell me what's wrong with it.”

## Autonomous companies, art, creativity, and generalists (Naval Podcast “The AI Industrial Revolution” Part 4 “The Autonomous Company”, nav.al/industrial, 2026-06-01)
Source: https://nav.al/industrial — first-party article transcript; Part 4 only (Parts 1-3 already covered via /tokens, /hardware, /regulatory); Naval-labeled turns only (guests Guillermo/Blake/Max and host Nivi not attributed).
- **Automate anything repetitive and live in your creative zone.** There's almost nothing left to automate in his own work; that's where he hopes everyone ends up. Repetitive job-career work is going away, which is scary, but you don't need a new idea daily, just one now and then that creates a point of leverage.
- **Software built by its users is coming, with caveats.** His vibe-coded app has an in-app bug queue that a daemon analyzes, fixes and ships to him as a TestFlight build. Letting users drive features unchecked gives you a Homer Simpson car.
- **Returns will be ~99% intelligence, 1% agency** (his counterpoint to "70% agency"): agents will exercise the agency; you make the smart decisions and tell them to implement, or even ask them what to build next.
- **Coding went ~100x, but 99% still won't code.** Vibe coding replaced his first-person-shooter gaming because it's as fun with a real output; most friends still see it as a black box. Normies play with video models more, which still lack taste and judgment.
- **Creativity is out-of-distribution surprise.** Stepping outside the system (Gödel's incompleteness) is the human edge. Once a style is everywhere (Studio Ghibli) it's in distribution and its art value is gone.
- **Art is conveyed emotion with intent.** The same object without intent is meaningless, so who made it matters; he credits the human behind a clever AI-generated image.
- **New ideas live outside language.** Training sets are huge, but physics, feeling, emotion and evolution aren't fully captured by language. Humans aren't dependent on pure randomness the way natural selection is; we prune infinite space. AI may be starting at the edges in bounded domains like math.
- **Human plus AI is the era, and it lasts longer than people think.** Human without AI, forget it; pure AI isn't there yet.
- **Higher productivity means hiring more, not fewer.** He wants to hire people who are smart, creative and good with AI more than ever, for leverage.
- **Generalists are having a field day; credentialism loses.** AI supplies base intelligence and domain knowledge, agents supply agency; what's left is creativity, taste and enough agency to start and stick with it. A PhD that only taught jargon gets cut through. Best move: get really good with the tools and keep knowing their moving edges.


## Recruiting, culture, and curating geniuses (Naval Podcast “Curate People” backfill, nav.al/curate-people, 2025-11-07)
Source: https://nav.al/curate-people (first-party transcript; only Naval-labeled turns used, Nivi's turns excluded).
- **Recruiting is the founder's job and can't be outsourced.** Founders can delegate everything except recruiting, fundraising, strategy, and product vision. The early people are the company's DNA; the company changes the moment the founder is no longer directly recruiting and managing everyone, not at some headcount number.
- **The best only want to work with the best.** Working with weaker people is cognitive load on great people. Test: let a candidate pull any team member aside for 30 minutes, and if they aren't impressed, don't join. The person you flinch at is the one to let go.
- **Intelligence, energy, integrity, plus low ego.** You can manage 30-40 low-ego people where you might manage five high-ego ones. Self-motivation matters too: "what did you get done this week?" is a manager's question, not a leader's.
- **You can't hire people better than you early on.** People better than you won't work for you for long; early, all you bring is you. Investors judge founders by who they can recruit.
- **Break every rule to get the best people.** Comp, cap table, location, title, hours, reporting line. HR and recruiters can't know which rules to break, and the best people aren't cogs.
- **Find undiscovered talent.** If you can spot talent from afar, so can everyone else; by the time someone's famous on Twitter it's too late. Pick an audacious mission, be early, source tinkerers at the edge. Makers have taste in other makers. Hire marketers and social people who can market themselves.
- **Great engineers are artists.** Art is anything done for its own sake and done as well as possible; Apple won as a company of engineer-artists (AirPods as the example).
- **Early teams look like cults, and should.** Mixing too many kinds of people regresses to a bland average; adding process or people as "automation" is the worst form of it.
- **Stay small; scale is the enemy at zero-to-one.** No Slack (it lets people asymmetrically waste each other's time); one-on-one communication, maker's time, people bored rather than busy.
- **Be opinionated; you can't make a product simple enough.** Every setting is an abdication of responsibility to the user. ChatGPT beat Google by being even simpler. Great founders are near-dictatorial; consensus advice implies you're weak, though bad founders are opinionated too.
- **The founder's personality is the company.** Your non-negotiables decide who you hire. He has extreme taste in investors (won't take money from just any VC) and in marketers and copy.
- **Iterations, not hours.** Learning comes from the number of test-and-guess loops; great people distill an insight from each one. Good teams throw away most of their work; pride is the biggest impediment in the idea maze, and a startup that keeps iterating stays ahead of a big company that copies it.
- **All new information starts as misinformation.**
- **Geniuses only.** Don't fill roles; if they're not a genius, don't hire them. Collect and warehouse geniuses when you find them, even without a slot. You're lucky to hire one a month. Fire mismatches, or you'll only recruit weaker people. Small companies shouldn't copy large-company HR, roles, or comp brackets.
- **You can't fix motivation.** Right person, wrong time happens. Burnout usually means the work isn't working or you don't enjoy it; when someone says "I'm burned out" he reads "I want to quit."
- **Fun = learning at the edge of capability** (after David Deutsch). Great people can't 80% something; he deletes 10,000-like tweets to fix a word, because the art being correct matters more than it being popular.


## Simplicity, deleting requirements, polymaths and physics (Naval Podcast “Find the Simplest Thing That Works” backfill, nav.al/simplest, 2025-10-02)
Source: https://nav.al/simplest (first-party transcript; only Naval-labeled turns used, Nivi's turns excluded).
- **Complex systems that work come from simple things iterated.** In AI, simple algorithms plus more data keep getting smarter; designing something complex up front and scaling it falls apart.
- **Product design is extracting the simple thing from the noise.** You iterate until you find what works, then remove what you added around it that you don't need. You have to remove things to get them to scale.
- **Question the requirement before optimizing.** Track every requirement to the individual who asked for it, ask if they really need it, delete what's unnecessary, then cut parts, and only then optimize, then chase cost and scale (Musk's method; the Tesla battery fiberglass-mat story).
- **Zero to one needs one person who holds the whole problem.** Usually the founder: they don't design each part, but know why each piece is there and what happens to the rest if one is removed.
- **Be a polymath, not a generalist.** "Generalist" is often a cop-out; a polymath can pick up any specialty to the 80/20 level to make smart trade-offs.
- **Study physics (or any STEM).** Physics teaches how reality works, is unforgiving, and beats the false beliefs out of you; social science can leave you with 10% real knowledge and 90% false. Basic physics is enough.
- **Tinkerers are at the edge of knowledge.** Builders using the latest parts (racing drones, fighting robots, home PCs) understand things best and advance knowledge fastest. If you're past choosing what to study, team up with them.

## Problems, knowledge, wealth and optimism — The Deutsch Files II backfill (nav.al/deutsch-files-ii, 2024-01-26; with David Deutsch and Brett Hall; Naval-labeled turns only)
Source: https://nav.al/deutsch-files-ii (Naval mostly asks questions here; only his own stated views and restatements he puts in his own words are listed, not Deutsch's answers).
- **Tools don't have their own problems.** An AI system's problem is imposed from outside; it's an instrument pointed at one narrow problem. Creative knowledge comes from an entity that has the problem itself: no problem, no solution, no new knowledge.
- **Knowledge is causal.** A problem needs a solution, the solution is reached creatively, and the resulting knowledge is a real thing that replicates itself into the world (GPS satellites, rockets), and in the multiverse framing forms a kind of crystal structure of knowledge.
- **Brute force won't find relativity.** Giving tools to blind search is "monkeys with calculators" instead of monkeys with typewriters; the combinatorics make it an impossibility.
- **Wealth follows from the right problems.** Knowledge plus physical assets cause transformations; with the right problems and solutions an entity grows wealth, and the creative leaps along the way grow knowledge, which grows more wealth.
- **Optimism from universality.** Inanimate nature has little diversity of knowledge and evolution grows it slowly; humans create knowledge far faster, and as universal explainers anything that can be explained, we can explain. We're at the beginning of an infinity of knowledge, so he's optimistic.
- **Motivated measurement.** If you want to believe something and drop your skepticism about the measurement (the room-temperature superconductor hunt), you can get almost any non-replicable result.
- **Anthropocentrism keeps sneaking back,** now dressed up as "the observer" in quantum interpretations.
- **Open problems.** We don't understand consciousness or creativity; we may understand how knowledge grows but not where it comes from.
- **Levels of explanation.** Some theories only emerge at higher levels (thermodynamics isn't visible in one molecule) and are valid at their own level; "it's all particle collisions" doesn't explain a murder. Good explanations should be relatively timeless rather than tied to initial conditions and the "t" variable.
